# English Version – Project Presentation

## Project Context

Our project focuses on an integrated ERP system for a small or medium-sized restaurant.

The system will centralize the main operational and administrative processes of the restaurant, including inventory, purchasing, sales, human resources, Food Costing, and CRM.

The main objective is to connect the information generated by these modules to improve operational control, cost analysis, profitability, and customer management.

---

## A. Problem Definition

The main problem is the lack of integration between the restaurant's operational processes, cost control, and customer management.

When inventory, purchases, sales, human resources, and customer information are managed separately, information can become fragmented or difficult to update.

In addition, the restaurant needs to know the real cost of its dishes and understand how changes in supplier prices affect profit margins.

It also needs to manage customer information, reservations, order history, loyalty, and satisfaction.

---

## B. Proposed Solution

The proposed solution is an integrated Enterprise Resource Planning system designed for a small or medium-sized restaurant.

The system will include the following modules:

- Inventory and Warehouse
- Purchasing and Suppliers
- Billing and Sales (POS)
- Human Resources
- Food Costing
- CRM and Customers

The Food Costing module will manage recipe technical sheets, calculate the cost of each dish, and generate alerts when changes in ingredient prices reduce profit margins.

The CRM module will manage customers, reservations, order history, loyalty, and satisfaction surveys.

The information from these modules will be connected to provide useful information for decision-making.

For example, the system could identify profitable dishes and relate them to customer preferences to support promotional and loyalty strategies.

---

## C. Justification

The project is valuable because restaurant processes are closely related to each other.

For example, a change in supplier prices can affect inventory costs, recipe costs, and the profit margin of a dish.

Likewise, sales and customer information can be used to identify popular products and support customer loyalty strategies.

Integrating these processes into an ERP provides a centralized view of the restaurant's operations and supports better decision-making.

---

## D. Final User

The main users of the system will be:

- Restaurant administrators
- Cashiers
- Waiters
- Kitchen staff
- Inventory and purchasing personnel
- Customer management personnel

Each user will have access to the functions required according to their role.

---

## E. Utility and Return on Investment

The potential return on investment can be achieved by reducing waste, manual errors, repetitive tasks, and unnecessary expenses.

Better inventory and purchasing control can also improve operational efficiency.

Food Costing can help identify dishes with low profit margins and support decisions about prices, suppliers, and the menu.

---

## ERP Advantages

- Centralized information
- Integration between processes
- Better inventory control
- Real-time recipe cost analysis
- Profit margin monitoring
- Improved customer management
- Reduction of manual tasks
- Support for decision-making

## ERP Disadvantages

- Development and implementation costs
- Employee training requirements
- Resistance to change
- Maintenance and updates
- Dependence on technological infrastructure
- Complexity of integrating different modules

---

## Existing Solutions

The project considers existing restaurant management solutions such as:

- Toast
- Lightspeed Restaurant
- Oracle MICROS Simphony

These solutions demonstrate that integrated restaurant management is already an established concept.

Our objective is not to replace these commercial platforms, but to develop a controlled-scope ERP for a small or medium-sized restaurant as an academic software architecture project.

---

## Project Differentiation

The main value of the proposal is the integration between restaurant operations, Food Costing, and CRM.

The system will connect:

**Purchasing → Inventory → Sales → Food Costing → Profitability**

and:

**Sales → Customers → Reservations → Order History → Loyalty**

This integration can provide a more complete view of the restaurant and support operational and commercial decisions.

---

## Conclusion

The proposed ERP aims to integrate the main processes of a restaurant into a centralized platform while connecting operational information with recipe costs, profitability, and customer management.

The project provides an opportunity to apply software architecture concepts to a real-world business context.
